A few thoughts ahead of our strategy session

How I think we
get to $100M.

I've spent some time trying to organize my thoughts ahead of our Thursday session — reading back through the plans we've written and the conversations we've had. I thought it was worth sharing in advance with everyone, rather than reacting live in a room. Plus, vibe coding is cool.

— Alex · shared with the leadership team

First, the important part: I'm in on the vision. A financial operating system that helps clients gain clarity and confidence over their business and personal finances — built on trust and deep relationships — is exactly the right place to be pointed. I'm not questioning where we're going. I want to offer a thought on how we get there — and I think it might be a little different from how we're running now.

Here's the picture I keep coming back to.

We've got a lot of tops spinning — and fast isn't always how you get there.
Slow is smooth·smooth is steady·steady is accurate

When everything's spinning that fast, all at once, it starts to wobble — I think we all feel it. The instinct is to spin faster. But spinning fast isn't always how you get where you're going. Sometimes the move is to steady what's already going, and get clear on what actually matters most.

The whole idea in one line

If we slow down just enough to steady the pace, we get smoother. Smoother lets us be accurate — and accuracy is what lets us navigate the market dynamics shifting under all of us.

This is not slowing down instead of growing. It's the fastest real path to $100M. And slow and steady isn't forever — we steady the tops for one focused period, and then we pick up speed. The inertia builds. A top spinning true carries momentum a wobbling one never can. The stabilizing is the on-ramp, not the destination.

What I'd propose

120–180 days to steady the ship.

One focused period with the primary goal of professionalizing and steadying the core business. Not instead of the vision — in service of it. Here's what that period is actually about, roughly in order of what matters most:

  1. 1

    Consistent governance and rhythm

    The single thing that would change the most is running the operating cadence we already designed — showing up to it, making decisions in it, and holding to them. We've written good plans before; where we struggle is follow-through. Rhythm is what turns smart conversations into governed execution.

  2. 2

    Finish what we've started before we layer on more

    Integration, systems, hiring, several rollouts landing the same week. Before we open new fronts, let's close the open ones. Every new initiative we start before finishing the last is part of why the tops wobble.

  3. 3

    The people layer

    We've said it ourselves more than once: talent is the root of most of what we're wrestling with. Development plans, clear ownership, and a steadier way to hire and grow people would relieve pressure across everything else.

  4. 4

    Clearly defined, aligned data

    We need to trust our own numbers and agree on where they come from — enough data governance that when we decide something, we're all working from the same reliable picture. Right now that isn't fully true, and it makes every other decision shakier than it needs to be.

The honest core of it

The biggest single unlock is concentrated leadership attention on the core for one focused stretch — and most of all from the founder / CEO / MP. The person at the top sets the pace of the whole firm; if the top of the org is moving fast between many things, everything under it wobbles with it. I think we need more of that attention pointed right at the center for this period. That's not a criticism — it's the opposite. The most valuable thing you can do for the vision right now is help us steady the base it has to stand on.

I believe we get there through greater collaboration and alignment across the ELT and the broader leadership team — not one or two of us pushing. Once we steady the ship, we work as a leadership team to build on this vision and create shared ownership — worth more than any single plan.

And where this discipline pays off

Steadying the base isn't just defense. It's what puts us in a stronger position for everything the vision needs later: better-aligned value across the firm, unlocked potential in the people and services we already have, better capital terms if and when the time comes to finance growth, and the room to set the right equity plan — one that rewards the team without creating drag on future growth. We make those big moves from strength, not from a wobble.

The parts of the vision I'm most excited to sharpen

None of this dims the vision.

The service model — trust over speed

  • Frame what we do around trust and deepening relationships — helping clients gain clarity and confidence over their business and personal finances. That's the durable thing when AI commoditizes the compliance work.
  • A small but real one: "client," not "customer." It signals the relationship we actually want.
  • Weight quality and timeliness over "speed." Speed for its own sake is how mistakes happen; clients value trusting the work and knowing it'll be there when they need it.

Let's define the big terms

"Proprietary AI" and "family office" are powerful ideas — but right now they're phrases more than plans. Let's define what we actually mean by each before we build on them.

On the bigger acquisition ambition

I love the ambition of a repeatable acquisition engine, and it's a real conversation — when we're closer to $75M in revenue. At our current size I don't think we're a capital allocator yet; the business isn't cash-rich enough to run acquisitions like a fund, and systematizing that now would spread us thin. So I'd gently suggest it's not part of this plan — it's a later plan we're deliberately growing toward.

One more, and a close

On capacity and talent

With Arianna coming on board, we're making a real investment in advisory leadership, and I'm glad we are. I'll just name gently that capacity and talent have been one of the harder, more important parts of the business — we've had some turnover, and that part of the business is being tested in a meaningful way right now. Worth going into this period with our eyes open on it, and treating it as one of the things a steadier rhythm is meant to protect.

Why I'm optimistic

Here's what actually gives me confidence: the thinking the firm has already done is genuinely good. The work at the end of last year and again this spring was strong. We don't have a strategy problem — we have a follow-through problem, and that's fixable with focus and rhythm rather than more brilliance.

So that's my whole point, really. Let's steady the tops. Let's get smooth. And from smooth, let's build real momentum — together — toward exactly the vision we all want.
