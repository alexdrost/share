01 — the business case

Why build this, in one page

Hall CPA's people decisions — hiring, promotions, retention, comp — currently rely on instinct, scattered spreadsheets, and whoever happens to remember. This system replaces that with a single source of truth and surfaces insights that directly drive firm performance.

The shift
Stop reacting to attrition. Start predicting it. Stop guessing at hiring quality. Start measuring it.
This isn't about having an HR database. It's about having the infrastructure to make the right call on people, consistently, before problems become crises — and to prove to partners and clients that Hall runs on data, not vibes.
01 / SEE

Visibility into what's happening

Every talent metric — headcount, attrition, eNPS, performance, comp — visible in real time. No more waiting for quarterly reports to discover a problem that started six months ago.

02 / PREDICT

Prediction of what's next

Flight risk scores update weekly. Cohort attrition patterns surface early warnings. Bench strength analyses show exactly where you're exposed if someone leaves tomorrow.

03 / ACT

Action on what matters most

When the right signal hits the right person at the right time, interventions become surgical. Retention conversations before resignation letters. Promotion discussions before offers elsewhere.

02 — the diagnosis

The problems we're already facing

This isn't a hypothetical system for a hypothetical problem. These are the exact issues surfaced in our recent internal conversations — and here's how Airtable addresses each one, along with the specific data points we'll track to know it's working.

Every issue below is drawn directly from recent leadership discussions about retention, hiring, performance, and onboarding. The pattern is consistent: we have the right instincts, but no infrastructure to act on them consistently. The six problem / solution pairs that follow translate each into a concrete Airtable build with measurable outcomes.

The underlying theme
“When the top two leaders don't have consistent 1:1s, things break down fast.”
— Internal leadership review

6 items · persistent
Recruiting

Reactive hiring, not proactive recruiting

The pain

Over-reliance on Indeed yields lower-caliber candidates. We need to “always be interviewing” A-players — especially after April 15 when we plan to upgrade four key roles — but no one owns a pipeline and no system holds passive candidates warm.

The Airtable fix

A Candidate CRM in the Talent base with pipeline status (Cold / Warm / In Conversation / Active), source tagging, and automations that ping recruiters quarterly for “stay-in-touch” follow-ups with high-value passive candidates. Source-to-hire attribution tied to eventual performance.

Data we'll track
  • Pipeline size by role family
  • Source-to-hire conversion %
  • Cost per hire by source
  • 12-month performance by source
  • Passive-candidate re-engagement rate
  • % of hires from “always on” pipeline
Retention

Critical-person concentration risk

The pain

Spencer is “paramount” — if he leaves, significant disruption. Other key people are equally exposed. There's no visible succession plan, no early warning on comp / equity / stability concerns, and transition plans are built in reaction, not prevention.

The Airtable fix

A Succession Planning table linking every critical role to ranked successors by readiness. Automations flag any role with zero “ready now” options. Retention Plans linked to flagged individuals capture comp structure, incentives, and equity path — with renewal reminders before they expire.

Data we'll track
  • Roles with zero ready successors
  • Avg. successor readiness score
  • Flight risk for top 10 critical
  • Comp vs band for critical roles
  • Retention plan coverage %
  • Critical-role tenure distribution
Leadership

Leadership gaps invisible until they erupt

The pain

Kaylin is viewed as inexperienced. Jen drives results but her team complains of workload and tone. Today, we rely on partner opinion to spot manager issues — objective signals arrive late, if at all. Misalignment at the top cascades into role-clarity problems below.

The Airtable fix

A Manager Effectiveness rollup composed of team eNPS, team attrition, goal attainment, 1:1 compliance, and team-workload pulse scores. Refreshed weekly from the Engagement and Performance bases. Separate “leadership cadence” tracker logs executive 1:1 completion.

Data we'll track
  • Manager effectiveness score
  • Team workload pulse
  • Team-level eNPS by manager
  • 1:1 compliance %
  • Executive cadence compliance
  • Team attrition by manager
Retention

Retention that's reactive, not proactive

The pain

People resign before anyone noticed the warning signs. The firm has had success with stay interviews in the past — but the practice isn't systematized and isn't applied to the right people at the right time. A “manager retention report” is in the works but lacks a consistent home.

The Airtable fix

Stay interviews become a first-class table in the Engagement base, scheduled on a quarterly cadence for top performers (via automation). Outcomes and concerns feed the Flight Risk model. High-risk individuals trigger automated alerts to their manager and People Ops.

Data we'll track
  • Stay interviews completed / due
  • Flight risk score trend
  • Regrettable attrition rate
  • Time-from-signal-to-intervention
  • Post-intervention retention %
  • Concerns raised by category
Onboarding

Ramp-up gap: 4 weeks promised, 3 months real

The pain

Capacity planning assumes four-week ramp. Reality is closer to three months. No 30-day assessment to catch early signals. New hires feel welcomed but drift without structured milestones — and managers don't know whether a new hire is on track until something breaks.

The Airtable fix

An Onboarding table with 30 / 60 / 90-day milestone records per new hire. Automated check-in forms to managers at each milestone. Ramp-up curves by role and department expose where onboarding is actually working and where it's not — so capacity plans reflect reality.

Data we'll track
  • % meeting 30 / 60 / 90-day milestones
  • Actual time-to-productivity by role
  • 90-day voluntary attrition
  • Hiring-manager satisfaction score
  • Ramp-up curve by department
  • % of new hires with completed check-ins
Hiring Quality

Culture fit, without the technical depth

The pain

New hires consistently fit the culture but lack the technical chops. That shows up later as under-performance, rework, and senior-time drain. Today there's no feedback loop from “interview score” to “actual performance at 12 months.” Our hiring rubric isn't learning.

The Airtable fix

Interview Scorecards capture separate culture and technical ratings with structured rubrics. A Hiring Quality rollup compares “score at hire” against “rating at 90 / 180 / 365 days” by interviewer and by source. Feedback loops inform which interviewers calibrate well.

Data we'll track
  • Technical at hire vs rating at 12mo
  • Hiring quality score by source
  • Interviewer calibration accuracy
  • % passing 90-day technical milestone
  • Re-work rate in first 180 days
  • Hiring manager post-hire NPS

05 — timeline & preparation

What it takes to get here from zero

Before the first Airtable base gets built, there's data to gather and decisions to make. Click through the six phases below to see what's involved, what we need from Hall, and a realistic sense of when each comes online. This isn't a project plan — it's the shape of the work.

6 phases · click through
Phase 0 · Before any Airtable work begins

Preparation & alignment

~3 weeks
What we need to collect
  • Current employee roster with hire dates, roles, managers, departments, and locations
  • Historical org changes (promotions, role moves, terminations) as far back as records allow
  • Job-description / role catalog
  • Any comp band definitions that exist today
  • Past survey results (eNPS, engagement) if any
  • Current HR tech stack inventory (payroll, ATS, survey tools)
Decisions to make
  • Who owns each base (Core HR, Talent, Performance, Engagement, Comp, Reporting)
  • Permissions model — who sees what, especially for comp data
  • Which domain base gets built first (Talent vs. Engagement)
  • Airtable Enterprise plan procurement & workspace setup
  • Standardized employee ID scheme (the single most important decision)
Deliverables
  • Data-export package ready to import
  • Signed-off permissions matrix
  • Stakeholder alignment on priority order
  • Airtable workspace provisioned
  • Kickoff meeting with base owners
Phase 1 · The foundation

Build Core HR — the source of truth

~4 weeks
What gets built
  • Employees table with full history-capable fields
  • Departments, Positions, Locations reference tables
  • Employment Events table (promotions, transfers, terminations)
  • Automation: log an event on every Position / Manager / Department change
  • Historical event backfill from prep-phase exports
What we need from Hall
  • Designated data owner for Core HR (likely People Ops)
  • Review & sign-off on field definitions
  • Validation that historical headcount reconstructs correctly
  • Access grants to People Ops team
Deliverables
  • Live Core HR base with all current employees
  • Historical org-change log reconstructed
  • First automation firing on real events
  • Foundational dashboard: headcount & attrition
Phase 2 · Prove the model

Ship the first domain base

~4 weeks
What gets built
  • One of: Engagement base (if stay interviews are priority) or Talent base (if April 15 hiring push is priority)
  • Sync from Core HR (one-way, read-only)
  • Full table schema per domain plan
  • First interactive Interface dashboard
  • Automations for the most valuable workflow (e.g., quarterly stay-interview reminders)
What we need from Hall
  • Domain data owner on standby for questions
  • Pilot user group (3–5 people) to give feedback
  • Willingness to adjust workflow based on what the data reveals
  • 2 hours per week of leadership review
Deliverables
  • Live domain base in daily use
  • Pilot group trained & using it
  • One production dashboard for that domain
  • Documented playbook to repeat for other bases
Phase 3 · Fill out the system

Roll out remaining domain bases

~8–12 weeks
What gets built
  • Performance Management base (reviews, goals, ratings, PIPs, promotions)
  • Second of: Talent or Engagement (whichever wasn't built in Phase 2)
  • Compensation base (restricted access, HR + Finance only)
  • Each base's Interface dashboards
  • Automations per base for the top-value workflows
What we need from Hall
  • Data owners for each new domain
  • Historical performance data (if we want rating history)
  • Comp band definitions — either existing or created during this phase
  • Clear access-control decisions for Comp base
Deliverables
  • All five source bases live and in production use
  • Five per-domain Interface dashboards
  • Core operational use in daily rhythm
  • Pass-off to ongoing ownership teams complete
Phase 4 · The payoff

Reporting & cross-base analytics

~4 weeks
What gets built
  • Reporting base with synced summaries from all 5 source bases
  • Flight Risk Scores — composite scoring with weekly refresh
  • Manager Health rollup table
  • Cohort Retention helper table (maintained by automation)
  • Executive Interface dashboards (the ones the CEO actually uses)
  • AI-generated interface elements for 9-box, heat maps, quadrants
What we need from Hall
  • Executive team review of flight risk weighting
  • Sign-off on which metrics appear where
  • Weekly dashboard review commitment
  • Feedback on what's useful vs. what's noise
Deliverables
  • Live executive dashboard
  • Flight risk scoring in production
  • All 8 signature analytics operational
  • Weekly dashboard cadence established
Phase 5 · Not a phase, a posture

Iterate & refine

Ongoing
What we'll be doing
  • Tuning flight risk weights based on actual outcomes
  • Expanding AI-generated dashboard elements as new use cases emerge
  • Adding fields and tables as questions surface
  • Retiring metrics that aren't driving decisions
  • Quarterly dashboard reviews to keep the system honest
Signals it's working
  • CEO & leadership open dashboards before meetings, not after
  • Retention interventions happen before exits, not during
  • Hiring decisions cite source-quality data
  • Succession conversations reference actual readiness scores
  • The Monday morning “what's happening” question has a single answer
Long-term considerations
  • At ~500+ employees, start evaluating warehouse-backed BI (Looker, Metabase) for heavy analytics while keeping Airtable as the system of record
  • Annual permissions / access audit
  • Sunset legacy HR spreadsheets as the system absorbs their role
Real-world context

On paper this is roughly 5 months of focused work to reach a fully operational system. In reality, with tax season, client deadlines, leadership bandwidth, and the mid-course adjustments every real rollout requires, a more honest calendar is 8 to 12 months end-to-end. The good news: you don't need to wait that long for value. Core HR plus one domain base is usable by month 3 and starts answering real questions immediately.
