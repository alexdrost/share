How I work

Eight things that shape how I handle almost anything

These will tell you more about how I'll approach something than a list of responsibilities would.

I show up with an ownership mentality

I don't really think about this in terms of scope. The question I ask is what it takes for something to work, not whether it happens to be mine.

I try to work on the business, not in it

My goal is to build the systems and information that help other people make good decisions and run the business, rather than making those decisions for them.

If it moves money, it's probably worth a conversation with me

That's a trigger for input, not a claim on the decision. The decision still belongs to whoever owns it.

I'll catch what gets dropped

This is something I can be uniquely useful on, especially with Tooth & Coin and anything on the corporate side. I'll get it to the right owner, and hold it myself when there isn't one yet.

Deliberate beats fast

A lot of what looks like a speed problem is really a clarity problem. Slow is smooth, smooth is steady, steady is accurate.

A sounding board for the leadership team

Most of the value I add shows up in conversation, before a decision, rather than in a document after it.

I try to systematize whatever I touch

Professionalization and repeatable process. If we're going to do something more than once, I'd rather it be a process than a heroic effort.

Write it down

Decisions, assumptions and who owns them, captured while they're still fresh. It keeps us from re-deciding the same things later, and it means the reasoning survives when the people in the room change.

Where I fit

Three circles

There's a version of this conversation that's about titles and reporting lines. I don't think that's the useful one. The useful one is about where three things overlap: what I'm good at, what the firm needs right now, and what I'd work on if nobody were watching.

What I own

Accountable and responsible are not the same thing

Most of the confusion about my role sits in the gap between those two words, so it's worth separating them before the lists make sense.

AccountableOne name. That person tracks it and reports on it.
ResponsibleDoes the work. Usually more than one person.
AuthorityMakes the call. Not always either of the above.

Bring me in

When it's worth pulling me into something

Roughly in the order I'd rank them. Earlier is always cheaper than later.

  1. A decision needs a model before it needs an opinion.
  2. A hire is on the table, or a role is being created or redesigned.
  3. Pricing is changing, or scope is changing in a way that affects pricing.
  4. A number has to hold up in front of leadership, a partner or a lender.
  5. A purchase, contract or renewal is material or multi-year.
  6. Two functions disagree and there's no forum to sort it out.
  7. Something on the Tooth & Coin side touches finance, systems or the transaction.
  8. You want a second brain on something hard, in confidence.

Earlier is cheaper.

Most of the times I've been useful, someone looped me in before the decision. Most of the times I haven't been, the decision was already made and I was being asked to bless it.
