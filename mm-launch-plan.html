Executive Summary

"The middle market is the most active and least well-served segment of U.S. private equity — and no independent media brand has claimed it."

This document is the operational launch plan for a portfolio of ten domain names positioned at the center of the lower and core middle market M&A ecosystem. The goal is to build a cash-flow media and professional intelligence business — not a venture-backed platform, not a build-to-flip domain play — through disciplined, phased execution starting with the minimum viable product and expanding as audience and revenue grow.

The portfolio is organized as a hub-and-spoke network. middlemarket.media is the flagship editorial brand at the center. The surrounding domains serve as content spokes (.news, .blog), community infrastructure (.network, .group), education and credentialing (.academy, .education), and commercial services (.solutions). Each has a defined role, a defined audience function, and a defined point in the launch sequence.

The Core Insight
ACG's Middle Market Growth is association-owned content that serves membership, not editorial goals. The Middle Market (themiddlemarket.com) is a 60-year-old trade pub that digitized. Axial and Grata write content to sell their platforms. No independent, newsletter-first, lower-middle-market media brand exists. This portfolio fills that gap.

Section 01 — Business Model

How the Business Makes Money

A diversified B2B media and education business with five revenue streams, each activating in sequence as the audience grows.

01

Business Model

The core insight is that the middle market deal professional — an independent sponsor, regional investment banker, or operating partner at a lower MM PE firm — is a scarce, high-earning, and commercially active audience that the sponsors who want to reach them (law firms, lenders, fund admins, insurance brokers) have large budgets and limited high-quality venues for. That scarcity is the business.

The model is the Axios playbook applied to a niche vertical: build a disciplined editorial brand, grow a loyal subscriber list, monetize through constrained sponsorship inventory, and layer in subscription and education revenue as the audience scales. Newsletter sponsorships exceeding 50% of revenue at lean operating cost is the target operating model for Phase 1.

Operating Cost Structure

The model is deliberately lean. Phase 1 operates with 1 full-time editor, 1 part-time contributor, and basic tooling — all-in burn of $12,000–$22,000/month. The AI-assisted deal wire production model (middlemarket.news) reduces daily production time to under 45 minutes, making the daily brief economically viable without a dedicated wire reporter. Costs scale with revenue: by Phase 3, a $50,000–$90,000/month burn rate supports $1.5M–$4M+ in revenue, implying strong EBITDA margins at scale.

The Flywheel
.blog content attracts pre-exit business owners and practitioners via organic search → they subscribe to .media and .news → engaged subscribers convert to .academy students and .network members → paying community members become the audience that sponsors and corporate clients pay premium rates to reach → revenue funds more editorial quality → editorial quality grows the list. Each revolution of the wheel reduces acquisition cost and increases revenue per subscriber.
Section 02 — Target Audience

Who We're Building For

Five distinct professional personas, each underserved by existing media and education infrastructure, each with strong commercial demand from endemic advertisers.

02

Target Audience

The middle market deal community is one of the largest and most commercially attractive professional audiences in American finance — yet it is treated as an afterthought by both Wall Street media (too small) and small business media (too sophisticated). The 4,000+ PE transactions per year in the middle market are executed by tens of thousands of professionals who receive almost no purpose-built editorial, education, or community infrastructure.

Primary Audiences — Build For These First
Primary · Tier 1
The Independent Sponsor
Ex-PE associate or IB VP going out on their own. Typically 32–45 years old. Has the deal experience but lacks institutional infrastructure: no committed fund, no dedicated admin, no internal training program. Sourcing deals deal-by-deal and raising capital on each transaction.
What they need Daily deal intelligence, capital provider relationships, structuring education, peer community, and credibility signals for sellers and LPs.
Primary · Tier 1
The Search Fund / ETA Operator
MBA graduate or career switcher pursuing entrepreneurship through acquisition. The fastest-growing buyer category in the lower middle market — search funds now account for 14% of Axial closed deals, an all-time high. Typically targeting $1M–$5M EBITDA businesses.
What they need How to structure a search, evaluate a target, raise capital, operate post-close. Heavily community-driven learning style. Need peers, not textbooks.
Primary · Tier 1
The Lower MM Deal Professional
Associates, VPs, and MDs at regional investment banks, boutique advisory firms, and lower MM PE firms ($100M–$500M AUM). Not at KKR — at a 15-person Midwest PE firm. Actively sourcing, diligencing, and closing deals but not well-served by Bloomberg or the WSJ.
What they need Deal comps, market intelligence, sector coverage, practitioner guides, and a professional community that doesn't require $1,500/yr ACG dues to access.
Primary · Tier 1
The PE-Backed Operating Executive
The CEO, CFO, or operating partner placed into or promoted within a PE portfolio company. Knows how to run a business — does not know how to navigate a PE board, build a value creation plan, or manage toward an exit. Chronically underserved by existing education and media.
What they need PE board dynamics, financial reporting for sponsors, 100-day planning, value creation frameworks, and a peer community of executives in the same situation.
Secondary Audiences — Grow Into These
Secondary · Tier 2
The Pre-Exit Business Owner
Founder or owner-operator of a business with $2M–$30M EBITDA, 3–7 years from an exit. Researching the M&A process online before engaging an advisor. This audience drives middlemarket.blog's SEO strategy and becomes the commercial engine for middlemarket.solutions.
What they need Independent, unbiased answers to: "What is my business worth? Should I sell to PE or a strategic? What does the process look like? How do I choose an advisor?"
Secondary · Tier 2
The Family Office Principal
Single and multi-family offices increasingly deploying capital directly into the lower MM. Family offices now close 30%+ of Axial deals, an all-time high. They want deal intelligence and advisor relationships but not full ACG membership infrastructure.
What they need Curated deal intelligence, intermediary access, and a vetted professional community. Lower volume than PE firms but longer relationship cycles and high lifetime value.
Why These Audiences Are Commercially Attractive

Every one of these professionals is actively spending on the services advertised by endemic sponsors. Independent sponsors need lenders, lawyers, fund administrators, R&W insurance, and deal sourcing platforms. Operating executives need technology vendors, HR platforms, financial advisory services, and board consultants. Pre-exit business owners need M&A advisors, attorneys, and accountants — and they're researching all of these online before making calls.

The Scarcity Premium
A newsletter reaching 10,000 lower middle market deal professionals commands sponsorship rates 3–5× higher than a general finance newsletter of the same size, because the audience is scarce, identifiable, and commercially active. Sponsors aren't buying impressions — they're buying introductions to the exact person who signs the check for their services.

The moat is not the domain names — those are a starting advantage, not a permanent one. The moat is the subscriber relationship built through consistent, trusted editorial over 18–24 months. If You're Off Track — Early Warning Signs
Open rate below 30% by Month 3: Content quality or audience targeting problem. Stop growing the list; fix the content first.

No sponsorship after Month 4: Pricing, audience size, or outreach approach needs adjustment. Lower the rate, broaden the sponsor category, or build a case study around your open rate data.

Academy waitlist under 30 by Month 10: Don't launch the first cohort until you have 30+ genuine waitlist signups. A half-full first cohort destroys the peer-learning dynamic that makes the product worth the price.
