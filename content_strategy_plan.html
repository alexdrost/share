The content intelligence system

Consistent content production requires a reliable intelligence system — knowing where to look, how often, and what to do with what you find. The sources below map to specific content types. Some are daily scan inputs for the newsletter. Others are quarterly triggers for deeper analysis. The database-powered sources (your 2,174 company websites) are covered in the next tab as a dedicated automated system.

Tier 1 — Primary deal flow sources (scan daily)

SEC EDGAR — Form 8-K and SC 13D filings Daily

Free. Companies must file 8-K within 4 business days of a material event including acquisitions. Full-text search at efts.sec.gov. Search "acquisition" filtered by filing date. Surfaces LMM deals that never get press coverage. Combine with SEC EDGAR's full-text search API (free) to automate deal detection. This is the most underused free deal sourcing tool in media.

PR Newswire / BusinessWire / GlobeNewswire Daily

Set up Google Alerts for "acquired by" + "private equity" + "lower middle market" and combinations thereof. Free tier catches most press release announcements. The limitation: only deals that issue press releases. Pair with EDGAR to catch unreported deals. Newswire RSS feeds can be pulled into a Make/Zapier automation to filter by keyword and pipe into an Airtable content tracker.

GF Data Monthly

The gold standard for LMM deal multiple data. Subscription required (~$2,500/year for full access). Publishes quarterly reports on EBITDA multiples, debt levels, and deal volume for transactions under $250M. Every Multiple Tracker post and Deal Intelligence report you write should cite GF Data. Worth every dollar — it's the data your audience considers authoritative.

Lincoln International Valuations Report Quarterly

Lincoln publishes free quarterly market pulse reports covering PE portfolio valuations. High-quality, specific to middle market. Download and mine each issue for newsletter and report data. They will also appreciate being cited — creates a relationship that could lead to contributed content or event partnership. Free.

Axial Deal Flow Data Monthly

Axial publishes periodic reports on deal flow activity on their platform (sector mix, buyer engagement, business sizes). Available on their blog. Useful for sourcing trend content. They are also a potential co-marketing partner — you serve the same audience in a complementary way.

PitchBook / Dealogic (free tiers) Weekly

PitchBook's free tier allows limited deal searches. Use to verify deal details found in other sources. For full access, PitchBook runs ~$30K/year — justify this cost once the newsletter has >5,000 subscribers and is generating sponsorship revenue. Until then, EDGAR + GF Data + Axial covers 80% of what you need at a fraction of the cost.

Tier 2 — Market intelligence sources (review weekly)

ACG Middle Market Growth Weekly

Monitor what ACG publishes. Where they're focused tells you where the audience is looking. Where they're absent or thin is your content opportunity. Especially watch their deal news and chapter event announcements — these surface active firms to reach out to.

The Middle Market (themiddlemarket.com) Weekly

Your closest direct editorial competitor. Review weekly to track what they're covering and how. Where their coverage is thin or generic is your differentiator. Their strength is breadth; your strength is depth on the LMM specifically.

Buyouts Magazine / PE Hub Weekly

Industry trades focused on PE. Watch for deal announcements, personnel moves, and fund news that affect firms in your database. Fund closes at PE firms in the database are a particularly good newsletter item — it signals active buying activity from a name your readers recognize.

LinkedIn company pages (database firms) Weekly

The 2,174 firms in the database all have LinkedIn pages. Their posts surface deal announcements, new hires, portfolio company news, and market commentary. This is one of the richest real-time signals for newsletter content — and it's free. The automated monitoring system in the next tab covers how to do this at scale.

Google Alerts (50 custom alerts) Daily

Set up 50 targeted Google Alerts: one per top PE firm in the database, plus keyword combinations like "lower middle market acquisition," "management buyout [sector]," "platform company [sector]." Free, runs automatically, sends digest email. Takes 2 hours to set up and saves 5 hours/week of manual scanning.

Reddit r/privateequity + r/investing Weekly

Underrated intelligence source. Real practitioners post questions and observations here. Trending topics in r/privateequity often surface before they appear in trade media — especially on operational topics, compensation, and career questions. Also reveals what content the junior audience (Associates/Analysts in the database) is actively searching for.

Layer 1 — Website RSS and news feed monitoring

1

Scrape RSS feeds from all 2,174 firm websites

Most PE and IB firm websites have a "News" or "Portfolio" section with an RSS feed or at minimum a news/blog page. Use a tool like Feedly Teams ($18/month) or Inoreader ($10/month) to subscribe to RSS feeds for all 2,174 sites simultaneously. Both tools allow bulk import from an OPML file — export company URLs from Airtable, build the OPML, import in one step. When a PE firm announces a new portfolio company, fund close, or deal, it surfaces immediately in your feed. This is how you catch deal news before it gets to trade press.

2

Build a Make.com or Zapier automation: RSS → Airtable content queue

When Feedly/Inoreader detects a new post from any monitored firm, trigger a Make scenario that: (1) pulls the article title and URL, (2) looks up the firm in the Airtable Companies table by domain match, (3) creates a new record in a "Content Queue" table with the article title, firm name, firm type, URL, and date. You now have an auto-populating content pipeline that requires no manual scanning. Cost: Make.com free tier handles ~1,000 operations/month; paid is $9/month for more volume.

Make.com scenario flow:
Watch Feedly feed → extract item.url + item.title + item.published
→ Extract domain from URL
→ Search Airtable Companies table where "Company Website URL" contains domain
→ If match found: Create record in Content Queue table
Fields: Article Title, Source URL, Company Name (linked), Firm Type (lookup), Date, Status = "Unreviewed"
→ If no match: Create record with flag "New Firm — Add to Database?"
→ Post Slack/email digest of new items each morning at 7am

Layer 2 — SEC EDGAR monitoring for closed deals

3

EDGAR full-text search API — detect acquisition filings by known firms

EDGAR's full-text search API (efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index) is free and returns all filings containing specific text. Build a weekly automated query that searches for acquisition-related language in 8-K filings filed by or mentioning any of the 521 IB firms or 880 PE firms in the database. Filings mentioning "[firm name] acquired" or "[firm name] completed the acquisition" surface deals that never issue press releases. This is original intelligence your competitors don't have.

4

Google Alerts batch — 50 targeted alerts for top firms

Google Alerts (free) monitors the web for new content mentioning specific terms. Set up 50 alerts — one for each of your top 50 PE firms by conference participation (use Recurring/Retained status from Airtable). Alert syntax: "[Firm Name]" acquisition OR deal OR portfolio OR fund. Alerts deliver to a dedicated Gmail address. Forward to an Airtable inbox via Zapier. Low-tech but catches news articles that EDGAR and RSS miss — particularly local business journal coverage of regional deals.

Save your 107 founding members and top power users as leads, and your top 200 companies as accounts. The daily feed surfaces new posts, deal announcements, and personnel moves from people you already know. This is also your most direct outreach trigger: when a power user posts about a deal, that's the moment to reply, engage, and deepen the relationship.

This section runs every week in the newsletter with zero manual writing. It also makes the featured firm feel noticed and recognized — which deepens the relationship and often generates a reply or a thank-you that turns into a BD conversation.

Layer 5 — PR and outreach automation from news signals

9

Deal congratulations outreach — triggered by deal news detection

Cost: the automation does the work; the relationship does the converting.

When a PE firm in the database announces a new fund close (a major signal of active deployment ahead), immediately move them to a sponsor outreach priority queue. A targeted sponsorship conversation within 2 weeks of a fund close announcement has a materially higher conversion rate than cold outreach at any other time.

Launch the KPI Benchmarks report in Q2 — operational metrics for portfolio companies by sector. Survey the community in March, publish in May. This is an annual email capture asset.

Healthcare Sector Report + Compensation Survey launch

Healthcare is the fastest-growing sector in the LMM (539 mentions). Q3 is the right timing for the Comp Survey — people are mid-year, thinking about performance reviews and year-end negotiations. Survey launches in July, results publish in September. This is your single highest email capture and inbound link event of the year.

Q4 (Oct–Dec)

State of the Lower Middle Market Annual Report

The year's most important content event. 20–30 pages of original analysis on the LMM deal environment, drawn from your 2,174-firm database, GF Data, EDGAR filings, and community survey data. Launch in November — when the PE year is wrapping up and everyone is reflecting on market conditions. This is what gets cited in deal memos, shared at conferences, and earns the backlinks and PR coverage that compound domain authority for the next year.
