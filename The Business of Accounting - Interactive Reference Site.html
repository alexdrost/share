Section 01

What Is a CPA Firm?

By the end of this section: I can explain how we make money.

The Firm Landscape

Big 4 & National
Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG + large nationals

Thousands of staff, Fortune 500 clients, global presence. Full service suite across all industries.

Regional Firms
$50M–$500M revenue, multi-office

Strong middle-market focus. Growing advisory and niche practices. Where much of accounting innovation happens today.

Local Firms
$5M–$50M revenue

Deep community roots. Often compliance-heavy with advisory emerging as a growth layer. The backbone of local business communities.

Boutique & Specialty ⭐
Fastest-growing model

Built around a specific niche — CFO services, CAS, a single industry vertical. Deep expertise commands premium pricing that generalists cannot match.

The Two Levers
① Revenue Growth
  • → Win more clients or expand existing relationships
  • → Move up the value chain: compliance → advisory
  • → Higher value commands premium pricing
  • → Niche specialization reduces price shopping
② Cost Efficiency
  • → Higher leverage ratio: more staff per partner
  • → Technology replacing low-value hours
  • → Standardize delivery to reduce per-engagement cost
  • → Fixed-fee pricing eliminates write-down risk
Your role: Every growth initiative should connect to one of these two levers. If it doesn't, you'll lose the budget conversation.

Who Leads the Firm

MP / CEO
Managing Partner / CEO
"CEO" vs "Managing Partner" signals how the firm views itself as a business. Sets strategy, owns culture, controls priorities. Often the gatekeeper for large decisions.
ExCom
Executive Committee
Strategic oversight and governance. Approves firm-wide decisions. Accountable to equity partners.
Equity
Equity Partners / Shareholders
Own the firm. Capital contributors. Profit share. Their income is directly tied to every dollar spent. Most protective of budget — because unspent dollars go into their pockets.
Non-Eq
Non-Equity / Income Partners
Partner title without ownership. Fixed comp. High influence, less formal power. Often excellent internal champions for new initiatives.
Staff
Directors, Managers & Staff
The delivery engine. The leverage pyramid lives here. Client work, billing hours, building expertise. Higher leverage = more profitable firm.

Section 03

Decision-Making & The Partner Model

By the end of this section: I know how to get something approved.

Know Who You're Talking To

Equity Partner
Owns the firm. Capital contributor. Profit share. Has a vote.
Approach: Frame every ask as ROI. Tie directly to revenue growth or cost reduction. Use peer firm examples and benchmark data.
Most protective of spend.
Non-Equity Partner
Partner title, no ownership. Fixed comp. On track to equity.
Approach: Cultivate as internal champion. They want to be seen as strategic — your initiative gives them a platform and a win to claim.
High influence, less formal power.
Senior Leader
COO, CFO, Director. High access, no equity-owner mindset.
Approach: Operational ally. Frame around efficiency, process, and scalability. They move faster than partners and often have pre-approval authority.
Key relationship to cultivate early.

The Tactical Playbook

✓
Start with the most influential partner, not the friendliest.
Find out who actually moves votes — not just who likes you. The friendliest partner rarely has the most leverage.
✓
Frame every ask around revenue or cost.
Tie it to the P&L. Speak the language of the room you're trying to influence.
✓
Win small things first to build credibility.
A track record of small wins buys you the credibility for bigger asks. Partners invest in people who deliver.
✓
Quantify everything.
"This campaign generated 3 new audits at $40K each." Attribution is hard — rough numbers still beat anecdote.
✓
Respect busy season — plan around it, not through it.
Trying to launch a campaign in February is like scheduling a sales call during a surgery. Know the calendar.
✓
Know which lever leadership emphasizes most.
Growth-focused leaders want BD fuel. Efficiency-focused leaders want ROI proof. Tailor your language accordingly.

What You're Taking With You

Things to Try This Week

The most valuable session takeaway isn't a framework — it's a habit. Pick one thing from each section and do it before your next partner meeting.

Your Commitments

Specific Things to Try

→
Ask your partner: "What's our realization rate?"
Then listen. What they say — and how they say it — tells you everything about their priorities and what kind of argument will land.
→
Map your firm's decision path for a major spend.
Write down who identifies the need, who approves it, who influences it informally, and where it typically stalls.
→
Align one active campaign to a revenue or cost lever.
Pick the one that's hardest to justify — and reframe it in P&L terms before your next internal conversation about it.
→
Ask a practice leader which service line has the most available capacity.
That's where BD investment has the best chance of converting. Capacity + demand = the best marketing ROI.
→
Use three terms from the glossary in your next partner conversation.
Utilization, realization, leverage ratio — use them naturally. The shift in how people respond to you will be immediate.

AI Prompts to Try

Paste these into Claude, ChatGPT, or any AI tool — then customize with your firm's specifics.

FIRM ECONOMICS

"Explain how utilization rate affects profitability in a mid-sized CPA firm, using simple terms a marketing director could use in a partner conversation."

BUSINESS CASE

TRANSLATE TO PARTNER LANGUAGE

"Rewrite this in a way that would resonate with a Managing Partner focused on margin and revenue growth: [paste your idea or proposal]."
